# POVODEŇ — Opening Video Script (English, draft v2 for review)

**Working title:** *"What the River Remembers"*
**Format:** stills + voiceover (Ken-Burns-style pans/zooms on generated images), documentary-interview frame
**Runtime target:** ~4:50–5:10 (see runtime note at the bottom — this grew significantly in v2; flagged for
your review, with trim options)
**Frame device:** a reporter from *The Elbe Herald* — the game's own in-world newspaper — interviews an old
riverman who lived through the 2002 and 2013 floods. Each of his answers becomes the lesson for one town OR
one game system. This doubles as the tutorial: every "mistake" he warns about maps to a real mechanic new
players get wrong.

**v2 changes from the first draft:** added dedicated beats for the forecast + sharpening + regional meeting
planning table, power cards + political capital, AI-mayor deals/relationships/favours, and — placed as the
emotional core, just before the closing thesis — the split between personal **re-election** and the region's
collective **score**. Existing town beats got small additions (Millington now covers Fun/celebration risk;
Oceana now ties into the rare-card research budget).

**Cast**
- **REPORTER — Petra Nováková**, young, notebook, calm curiosity. (Not used elsewhere in-game — safe to reuse
  the *Elbe Herald* branding from the in-game newspaper.)
- **OLD FOJTÍK — Antonín Fojtík**, late 70s, retired ferryman/rescue-boat pilot. Weathered, dry humor, speaks
  in short declaratives. He is *not* one of the seven playable mayors — he outlived several of them.

**Visual language:** desaturated sepia/blue archival stills for his memories (the 2002/2013 floods), warmer
present-day color for the interview frame. Each beat gets ONE hero still. Keep composition simple — these
will be Ken-Burns pans, not full scenes.

## 01 — THE INTERVIEW BEGINS

| | |
|---|---|
| **VISUAL** | STILL 1 — A small kitchen table by a window, river visible outside, two mugs, afternoon light. An elderly man (FOJTÍK) sits across from a young woman with a notebook (NOVÁKOVÁ). |
| **REPORTER** | "Mr. Fojtík — you pulled people out of the water in 2002. Again in 2013. If a new mayor sat down at this table today, what would you tell them?" |
| **FOJTÍK** | "First? That every euro you spend, and every euro the flood costs you — it's the same coin. Spend it wisely before the water comes. Because afterward, you're just counting the damage in the money you didn't spend." |
| **REPORTER** | "And after that?" |
| **FOJTÍK** | "That the river doesn't forgive arithmetic. It forgives *neighbors*." |
| **REPORTER** | "What do you mean?" |
| **FOJTÍK** | "Sit. I'll go town by town. Seven of them, one river. Same seven mistakes, every generation. And after the towns — I'll tell you what the water taught mayors about cards, promises, and elections. That part matters just as much." |

## Runtime note — please decide

v1 was ~2:48. Covering every system pushed v2 to **~4:50–5:10** of spoken material. That's long for a
"cold open before the menu" experience. Options, roughly in order of how much rework each needs:

1. **Keep it as one ~5-minute video**, but make it *skippable* and reachable again later from the menu (e.g.
   a "Watch the Interview" button next to How to Play) rather than forced every time. Zero script changes.
2. **Split into two videos**: Part 1 = the seven towns (scenes 00–02, 04–07, 11, 13–14 minus systems, ~3:00),
   Part 2 = "The Mayor's Office" covering forecast/cards/deals/re-election (scenes 03, 08–10, 12, ~2:00),
   watchable separately from the menu. Needs a small connective rewrite at the seams.
2. **Trim in place**: fold 03+08 together and 09+10 together (forecast/meeting with cards; Oceana with
   promises) to cut ~45–60s while keeping every lesson, at the cost of pacing/breathing room.
3. **Cut something**: least-essential candidates are 10 (promises/deals — rich but the longest new beat) or
   splitting 12 out as a *separate*, later "twist" video shown after the player's first campaign ends rather
   than before it starts (arguably even *more* effective dramatically, since they'd have just lived it).

My instinct is **option 4 restructured slightly**: keep 00–11+13–14 as the ~4:00 pre-game intro (still long,
but every beat is a town or a control they'll touch in the first minute of play), and hold back scene 12 (the
mayor who won the election) as a **separate ~30s video shown once, right before their first final briefing**
— which is exactly where the game already delivers that twist mechanically. That would also shorten the
"cold open" back toward v1's length. Tell me which way to cut it, or if 5 minutes flat is genuinely fine for
your use case (e.g. a facilitated workshop where someone introduces the game anyway).
